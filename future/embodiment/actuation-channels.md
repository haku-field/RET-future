# Actuation Channels

This document defines **actuation channels** within **RET-future**.

Actuation channels describe **paths through which signals could result in effects**.
They are defined to **limit and bound** such paths, not to enable them.

Actuation here is treated as a **structural possibility**, not an intention or action.

---

## Purpose

Actuation channels exist to:

- Explicitly enumerate where effects *could* occur
- Prevent hidden or implicit action paths
- Separate detectability from effect
- Protect RET from unintended intervention

They are written to **constrain**, not to empower.

---

## What an Actuation Channel Is

In RET-future, an actuation channel is:

- A defined path from signal presence to potential effect
- Describable without agency or intent
- Bounded by explicit constraints
- Optional and non-mandatory

An actuation channel is **not**:

- A command pathway
- A response mechanism
- A control loop
- A behavioral trigger

Possibility does not imply use.

---

## Default Stance: Inactive

All actuation channels are **inactive by default**.

- No channel is assumed to be enabled
- No channel is assumed to be safe
- No channel is assumed to be desirable

Activation, if any, is always **external to RET-future**.

---

## Channel Properties

Actuation channels may be described by:

- **Directionality**  
  One-way, non-reciprocal paths only

- **Latency**  
  Delay between signal and potential effect

- **Scope**  
  Localized or bounded effect domains

- **Irreversibility**  
  Whether effects can be undone

These properties describe **risk surfaces**, not capabilities.

---

## Prohibited Channel Characteristics

Actuation channels must **not**:

- Form feedback loops
- Create autonomous behavior
- Enable optimization or learning
- Escalate signal presence into obligation
- Introduce control, governance, or enforcement

Any channel that self-amplifies is invalid.

---

## Separation from Signals

Signals and actuation must remain **strictly separated**.

- Signals do not request action
- Signals do not justify action
- Signals do not authorize action

Actuation channels exist despite signals, not because of them.

---

## Non-Interference Requirement

Actuation channels must not:

- Modify RET layers
- Write back into condition sets
- Affect evaluation criteria
- Alter signal definitions

Any such interaction constitutes interference.

---

## Failure Conditions

An actuation channel triggers failure if:

- It becomes implicitly active
- It is treated as default behavior
- It is framed as necessary for evaluation
- It introduces agency attribution
- It enables intervention during evaluation

Failure terminates evaluation immediately.

---

## Documentation Rule

Each actuation channel entry must include:

- A literal description of the possible effect path
- Explicit bounds and limits
- Clear statement of non-activation by default

Language must avoid verbs implying intention or choice.

---

## Summary

Actuation channels in RET-future are:

- Explicitly bounded
- Inactive by default
- Non-agentive
- Non-authorizing

They exist to make **possible effects visible**
so they can be **constrained or rejected**.

If an actuation channel begins to feel useful,
evaluation has already crossed the boundary.
