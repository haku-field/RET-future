# Sensor Surfaces

This document defines **sensor surfaces** within **RET-future**.

A sensor surface is a **detection interface** on a carrier
through which signals may be observed.

Sensor surfaces are **not perceptual organs**.
They are structural detection points.

---

## Purpose

Sensor surfaces exist to:

- Describe where signals become detectable
- Separate detection from interpretation
- Constrain observation without implying awareness
- Enable comparison across carrier models

They support evaluation, not perception.

---

## What a Sensor Surface Is

In RET-future, a sensor surface is:

- A defined interface for signal detection
- Describable without reference to experience
- Bounded by measurable limits
- Passive by default

A sensor surface is **not**:

- A sense
- A perception channel
- An awareness boundary
- An interpretive layer

Detection does not imply understanding.

---

## Surface Properties

Sensor surfaces may be described by:

- **Sensitivity**  
  Thresholds at which signals become detectable

- **Resolution**  
  Granularity of detectable change

- **Latency**  
  Delay between signal occurrence and detection

- **Noise tolerance**  
  Capacity to register signals amid interference

These properties are descriptive, not evaluative.

---

## Surface Locality

Sensor surfaces are **local to carriers**.

They must not be treated as:

- Global observers
- System-wide monitors
- Aggregating authorities

Local detection does not grant global visibility.

---

## Non-Intervention Constraint

Sensor surfaces must remain **non-intervening**.

They must not:

- Modify signals
- Trigger responses
- Initiate actions
- Optimize detection behavior

Detection ends at registration.

---

## Relationship to Timing and Synchronization

Sensor surfaces may affect:

- Detection timing
- Observed alignment or misalignment
- Apparent absence due to thresholding

These effects are structural artifacts,
not behavioral signals.

---

## Isolation from Interpretation

Sensor surfaces must not be mapped to:

- Perception
- Attention
- Salience
- Awareness
- Preference

If detection is treated as perception,
the abstraction has failed.

---

## Documentation Rule

Descriptions of sensor surfaces must:

- Remain interface-focused
- Avoid experiential language
- Avoid anthropomorphic framing
- Avoid purpose attribution

If a surface begins to “notice,”  
evaluation must stop.

---

## Summary

Sensor surfaces in RET-future are:

- Detection interfaces
- Passive
- Non-intervening
- Non-perceptual

They define **where signals appear**,
not **how they are experienced**.

If detection becomes perception,
the boundary has been crossed.
