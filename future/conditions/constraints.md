# Constraints

This document enumerates **constraints** within **RET-future**.

Constraints define **hard boundaries** that restrict or invalidate evaluation,
regardless of assumptions or unknowns.

A constraint is a **limit**, not a challenge.

---

## Role of Constraints

Constraints exist to:

- Delimit the possible evaluation space
- Terminate infeasible condition sets
- Prevent reinterpretation of impossibility as difficulty
- Protect RET from contamination through overreach

Constraints are **decisive**, not provisional.

---

## Nature of Constraints

All constraints are:

- Non-negotiable within their scope
- Independent of desirability or intent
- Not subject to optimization
- Capable of invalidating entire condition sets

A single violated constraint is sufficient to halt evaluation.

---

## Allowed Constraint Types

RET-future constraints may include:

- Physical or computational impossibilities
- Irreducible latency or synchronization limits
- Incompatibility between carrier substrates
- Non-intervention violations
- Forced optimization or control pressure
- Irreversible contamination of RET layers
- Loss of isolation between parallel layers
- Mandatory human-facing interpretation requirements

Constraints may originate from:

- Structural limits
- Logical contradictions
- External system requirements
- Irreversible coupling effects

---

## Disallowed Constraint Framing

Constraints must **not** be framed as:

- Risks to be mitigated
- Trade-offs to be balanced
- Problems to be solved
- Engineering challenges
- Design targets

If a constraint invites effort, it has been miswritten.

---

## Relationship to Assumptions and Unknowns

Constraints differ fundamentally:

- Assumptions may fail quietly
- Unknowns may persist indefinitely
- Constraints **terminate evaluation**

Constraints do not depend on assumptions or unknowns to activate.

---

## Constraint Precedence

When multiple condition types interact:

1. **Constraints override all**
2. Assumptions are evaluated only if constraints hold
3. Unknowns remain inert unless referenced

No condition may bypass a constraint.

---

## Documentation Rule

Each constraint entry must include:

- A concise statement of the limiting condition
- The scope of invalidation
- Whether the constraint is absolute or contextual

Constraints must be written **without workaround language**.

---

## Removal and Revision

Constraints may be:

- Revised
- Narrowed
- Removed

But only through **external justification**.

Internal reinterpretation is prohibited.

---

## Summary

Constraints in RET-future are:

- Hard limits
- Evaluation stoppers
- Non-negotiable
- Non-optimizable

They exist to say **“no”** without explanation.

If a constraint can be reasoned away,  
it was not a constraint.
