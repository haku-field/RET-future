# Sufficiency

This document enumerates **sufficient conditions** within **RET-future**.

A sufficient condition is a condition set that, **if satisfied**,  
allows evaluation of RET extensibility to proceed **without implying success or desirability**.

Sufficiency enables evaluation.  
It does not justify extension.

---

## Role of Sufficient Conditions

Sufficient conditions exist to:

- Define when evaluation *may* proceed
- Prevent unnecessary termination once minimum coherence is met
- Separate feasibility from desirability
- Allow comparison between alternative condition sets

They answer one question only:

> *Is evaluation permitted to continue?*

---

## Nature of Sufficient Conditions

All sufficient conditions are:

- Enabling, not authorizing
- Context-bound
- Non-exclusive
- Non-guaranteeing

Meeting a sufficient condition does **not** imply:

- Correctness
- Stability
- Safety
- Adoption
- Promotion into RET

---

## Relationship to Other Condition Types

Sufficient conditions are evaluated:

1. **After constraints are satisfied**
2. **After necessary conditions are met**
3. **Alongside unknowns (without resolving them)**

Sufficiency does **not** override necessity or constraints.

---

## Composition of Sufficiency

Sufficient conditions may be expressed as:

- Single conditions
- Explicit condition sets
- Alternative bundles allowing parallel evaluation

Sufficiency may be **plural**.

Multiple sufficient sets may coexist without competition.

---

## No Optimization or Ranking

RET-future does **not**:

- Rank sufficient conditions
- Compare them by efficiency
- Optimize toward easier satisfaction
- Select a “best” path

Sufficiency is binary **per set**.

---

## Failure Within Sufficiency

If a sufficient condition later fails:

- Evaluation halts
- No fallback sufficiency is auto-selected
- No repair is suggested

Failure does not retroactively affect other condition sets.

---

## Documentation Rule

Each sufficient condition entry must include:

- A clear description of the enabling condition
- The evaluation scope it permits
- Explicit statement of what it does **not** guarantee

Language must avoid implication of progress or approval.

---

## Removal and Mutation

Sufficient conditions may be:

- Revised
- Split
- Replaced
- Removed

Their existence does not accumulate authority.

---

## Summary

Sufficient conditions in RET-future are:

- Permission gates
- Non-authoritative
- Non-exclusive
- Non-predictive

They allow evaluation to proceed  
without committing RET to any outcome.

If sufficiency begins to resemble endorsement,  
it has exceeded its role and must be removed.
