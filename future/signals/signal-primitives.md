# Signal Primitives

This document defines **signal primitives** used within **RET-future**.

Signal primitives are the **minimal units** by which non-verbal interaction
is represented as **signals**, not experiences, meanings, or actions.

They exist to enable evaluation, not interpretation.

---

## Purpose

Signal primitives serve to:

- Represent interaction without narrative
- Separate signaling from intention or experience
- Enable timing and synchronization analysis
- Support machine-level comparison and inspection

Signals here are **descriptive markers**, not communicative acts.

---

## What a Signal Is (Here)

In RET-future, a signal is:

- A detectable occurrence or transition
- Representable without semantic content
- Observable without intervention
- Comparable across substrates

A signal is **not**:

- A message
- An intention
- An expression
- An experience
- A request or response

---

## Primitive Properties

All signal primitives share these properties:

- **Minimality**  
  No internal structure beyond what is required for detection.

- **Substrate-agnosticism**  
  Independent of device, body, or medium.

- **Non-agentive form**  
  Signals do not imply actors or senders.

- **Non-semantic stance**  
  No meaning is inferred or assigned.

---

## Core Primitive Types

The following primitive categories may be used.
They are **types**, not values.

### Presence

Indicates detectable existence without change.

No duration, intention, or awareness is implied.

### Transition

Marks a change between states or phases.

The nature of the change is unspecified.

### Timing

Represents ordering, delay, or simultaneity.

Timing carries no evaluative weight.

### Synchronization

Indicates alignment or misalignment between signals.

Synchronization does not imply coordination.

### Absence

Represents detectable non-occurrence within a defined window.

Absence is treated as a signal, not a failure.

---

## No Derived Meaning

Signal primitives must **not** be combined to derive:

- Intent
- Emotion
- Compliance
- Engagement
- Success or failure

Any such derivation exceeds the scope of RET-future.

---

## Relationship to Other Layers

Signal primitives:

- May be referenced by condition sets
- May be used in timing or taxonomy documents
- Must not propagate into RET protocols or fragments

They remain **local to RET-future**.

---

## Documentation Rule

Each primitive definition must:

- Remain literal
- Avoid metaphor
- Avoid experiential language
- Avoid implication of agency

If a primitive requires explanation to justify its relevance,
it is misdefined.

---

## Summary

Signal primitives in RET-future are:

- Minimal
- Non-semantic
- Non-agentive
- Comparable

They provide a way to **mark interaction**
without turning it into communication.

If a signal begins to tell a story,
it is no longer a primitive.
